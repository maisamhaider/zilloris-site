Motherword — Privacy Policy

App: Motherword (com.zilloris.motherword)
Updated: 27 October 2026
Contact: support@zilloris.com

The short version

Everything you keep in Motherword stays on your phone: the words you take back, the recordings of your family, the names you give the people in them, and your settings. There is no account, no sign-in and no sync. There is no analytics, no advertising and no advertising identifier, and nothing is sold or used to profile you.

The app uses the network for one thing: asking Google Firebase for the addresses of this policy and the terms, and downloading those two pages from zilloris.com so what you read is the text in force today. No recording and no word you kept ever travels with that.

What the app stores, and where

The words you have taken back and when; the recordings you make in the app, and the voice notes you share into it from WhatsApp or another app, which are copied into the app's own private storage on your phone; the names you give the people who speak in them; and your settings: your home language, your text size and your choice of dialect.

All of it is in the app's private storage on your device. There is nothing to sign in to, and no copy of any of it held by us. We have no server for it to be held on.

What leaves the device

What you send yourself. The family book can hand a recording to Android's own share sheet, and the elders' screen can hand the week's question to WhatsApp or another messaging app. You choose where each goes, and once it is there it is under that app's or that service's terms, not this one's. Those are the only ways a recording or a question leaves the phone.

And the configuration request and the two page downloads described next. Nothing else. No recording is ever uploaded by the app.

Configuration and the two documents

To know where this policy and the terms live, the app asks Firebase Remote Config for two addresses. That request carries a random installation identifier and the standard technical details such a request carries: the app version, your device model, your language, your country and your network address. What comes back is two addresses and nothing else. Firebase is run for us by Google LLC, which processes this data on our behalf under Google's privacy policy.

The two documents themselves are published at zilloris.com, on GitHub Pages, a GitHub service. When you open either one inside the app, the app downloads that page. It is an ordinary web request, carrying the page's address and — like any web request — your network address. Nothing of yours goes with it.

The app contains no analytics and sends no crash reports. It counts nothing about how you use it.

Permissions

Microphone: used only while you record, in the elders' screen or when you hold the button on a word card to say the word yourself. The phone asks you the first time, and declining only means the app cannot record.

Notifications: one reminder a day, at a time you choose in Settings, only on days your words are still waiting. The phone asks you the first time; declining only means no reminder. Every reminder is set by your phone and created on your phone: there is no push messaging service and no message from a server. The app also asks to be told when the phone restarts, so it can set the reminder again.

Network: used only for the configuration request and the two documents described above.

Recording other people

The recordings are of your family, made by you. Ask the person before you record them, and tell them it stays on your phone. The app keeps the recording only there, and shares it only when you choose to.

Backup

If you have Android backup switched on, Android may copy this app's data, recordings included, into your own Google Drive. It is your Drive and your Google account, not ours: we have no account, no server, and no way to see it. You can turn that off in Android's own backup settings.

Deleting your data

Deleting the app deletes everything it stored on your phone: your kept words, your recordings, the names and your settings. We hold no copy anywhere. The only copies that can remain are the ones that are yours: a recording you shared out of the app, which stays where you sent it, and Android's own backup, if you have it switched on, which is removed through Android's backup settings.

Children

Motherword is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone.

Changes

If this policy changes, the updated date above changes with it. The app downloads this page when you open the policy inside it, so it shows the current text; the copy built into the app is only what it falls back to when there is no connection.
