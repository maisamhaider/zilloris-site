Using the app

Arkiv keeps your household's papers on your phone — passports, ID cards, licences, car papers, insurance — reminds you before they run out, and makes PDFs of them to share or print. It is free to download, has no ads and has nothing to buy. There is no account: installing it is the whole of it.

Arkiv is not directed at children under 13.

Your papers are yours

What you put into Arkiv belongs to you, and you are responsible for what you add and who you share it with. We claim no ownership of it, and we never receive it: the app never uploads your papers, and we have no server for them to be sent to. Because we never receive them, we cannot read them, recover them or hand them to anyone.

Reminders are a help, not a guarantee

Phones can delay or stop an app's reminders, and a date read from a paper can be read wrong. Check the dates Arkiv shows against the papers themselves. Renewing on time stays your responsibility.

Backups are yours to keep

A backup file opens only with the password you chose for it. We cannot recover a lost password, and we cannot bring back a lost phone's papers without a backup. When you keep a backup in your Google Drive or anywhere else, that place's own terms apply to the file.

No warranty

Arkiv is provided as it is. To the extent the law allows, Zilloris is not liable for missed renewals, lost data or other losses from using it. Nothing here removes rights you have as a consumer that cannot be excluded under the law where you live.

Services we do not run

Google Play distributes the app and its updates, under Google Play's terms. Google Firebase supplies the settings for the app and for its speed-timing part (Remote Config). Each time it asks, the app sends a random installation identifier and technical details about your phone and the app, such as versions, phone model, language and time zone, but never a paper. Only if you say yes does Firebase also receive crash reports, usage counts and speed timings. Google's ML Kit, built into the app, reads the text on your phone. It sends Google short notes on each scan and each reading, such as how long it took, how many pages and any error, never the picture or the text, whatever you answer. Google's document scanner, part of Google Play services, runs the scanning screen. If you put reminders in a calendar, that calendar's provider keeps them under its own terms. Google Drive, or whichever app holds your backup, stores it under its own terms. GitHub Pages publishes these pages at zilloris.com; they open in your browser. What each one involves is described in the privacy policy.
