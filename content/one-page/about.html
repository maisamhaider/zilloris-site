One page a day. Two minutes. Your whole backlog, actually finished.

You have fourteen unfinished books. One Page gives you exactly one page a day — a pace so small you can't lose to it, and steady enough that “someday” turns into a finish date you can see.

Bring your own books

Open an EPUB from your phone and One Page picks up where you left off. The file stays on your device. Your books are never uploaded.

No EPUB to hand? Ten classics whose copyright has expired come built in — tap one and your first page is ready, with nothing to download.

Find any book

Reading a paperback, a Kindle book or a library copy? Search for it, add it, and read your own copy. One Page shows the page for the day and keeps your place.

Read one page. That's it.

No thirty-minute goals to abandon by Thursday. Set your pace, and one page is waiting for you each day. The finish date is arithmetic, not a promise: your pace, your book, your page count.

A streak with no guilt trips

Your streak is a record, not a hostage. Up to three reminders a day, at times you choose — none on a day you have already read, and never a passive-aggressive one at 11pm.

What stays yours

No account to make and nothing to buy inside the app. What you read stays on your phone. The app asks before sending anything: if you agree, crash reports, anonymous usage counts and performance timings go to Google Firebase, so bugs get found and fixed. The details are in the privacy policy.
