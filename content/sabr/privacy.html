Sabr — Privacy Policy

App: Sabr (com.zilloris.sabr)
Updated: 24 September 2026
Contact: support@zilloris.com

The short version

Your habits stay on your phone. Your line names, your daily amounts, your pen lifts, your holds and your settings are stored on your device and are never uploaded. There is no account, no sign-in and no sync.

The app makes one kind of network request, and only one: it downloads this policy and the terms of use from zilloris.com, so that what you read is the text in force today, and it asks Google's Firebase Remote Config where those two documents live. Nothing you write in the app goes with either. There is no analytics and no crash reporting.

Android's own backup, described below, can copy this app's data into your own Google Drive if you have device backup switched on.

What the app stores, and where

Your lines and what you named them, the amount you recorded each day, the days you lifted the pen, the days you put on hold, and your settings are stored in the app's own private storage on your device.

There is no account to make, nothing to sign in to, and no copy of any of it held by us. We have no server for it to be held on.

What leaves the device

What you send yourself. Settings offers an export of your page as a CSV file, handed to Android's own share sheet. You choose where it goes — a mail app, a file, a cloud folder — and once it is there it is under that app's or that service's terms, not this one's. That export is the only way your habit data leaves the phone.

And the request described in the next section. Nothing else. In particular, your line names, your daily amounts, your pen lifts and your holds are never transmitted by the app.

The one request the app makes

This policy and the terms of use are published at zilloris.com, on GitHub Pages, a GitHub service. When you open either one inside the app, the app downloads that page so you read the current text rather than whatever was true when the build was made. It is an ordinary web request, carrying the page's address and — like any web request — your network address. Nothing of yours goes with it.

To know which two addresses to ask for, the app asks Google's Firebase Remote Config. That request carries a Firebase installation identifier and the standard technical details such a request carries: the app version, your device model, your language, your country and your network address. It is not linked to a person, and the app puts none of your habit data into it. What comes back is two addresses and nothing else.

Firebase is run for us by Google LLC, which processes this data on our behalf under Google's privacy policy. There is no account, so none of it can be looked up by who you are.

The app contains no analytics and no crash reporting. It counts nothing about how you use it, and reports nothing when it breaks.

Permissions

Notifications, if you allow them, are used for exactly one optional daily reminder, at the time you chose. It is scheduled by your phone and created on your phone: there is no push messaging service and no message from a server. Declining costs you nothing else in the app, and you can turn it off in Settings or in Android at any time.

The app also holds Android's ordinary network permission, used only for the request described above, and by Google Play on this phone to ask whether a newer version of this app exists so an update can be offered — a check that carries nothing about your habits.

Backup

If you have Android backup switched on, Android may copy this app's data into your own Google Drive. It is your Drive and your Google account, not ours: we have no account, no server, and no way to see it.

You can turn that off in Android's own backup settings, and the app will not mind.

Deleting your data

Deleting the app deletes the page. Uninstalling Sabr removes everything it stored on your phone — your lines, your days, your pen lifts, your holds and your settings — and nothing comes back on reinstall, because there is no copy anywhere else. That is why Settings offers the CSV export: take one first if you would be sorry to lose the record.

A copy already taken by Android backup is removed through Android's backup settings, since that copy is in your Drive and not somewhere the app can reach.

Children

Sabr is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone.

Changes

If this policy changes, the updated date above changes with it. The app downloads this page when you open the policy inside it, so it shows the current text; the copy built into the app is only what it falls back to when there is no connection.
